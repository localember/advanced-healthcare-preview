The short answer

The first visit is a detailed history and a full exam that normally takes an hour, not a quick adjustment off a short form.

What causes low back pain?

Most low back pain is mechanical: it comes from how the joints, discs, muscles, and nerves of the spine are moving and loading, rather than from a serious disease. In Fort Collins, that often starts with everyday strain, from yard work and raking leaves in the fall to scooping snow in winter. It may have started yesterday, or years ago.

Everyday strain, a stretch of poor posture, a weak or guarded core, or a single awkward lift can leave the low back stiff, irritated, and slow to settle on its own. The usual contributors we see in Fort Collins families include:

  • Prolonged sitting and desk work that shortens the hips and loads the low back.
  • Lifting and twisting under load, from moving day to a toddler on one hip.
  • Weak or deconditioned core muscles that leave the spine doing work they should share.
  • Repetitive strain from a job, a sport, or a hobby done the same way for years.
  • Old injuries that changed how you move and quietly overload one area.

Less often, back pain points to something that needs a medical work-up first. Part of a good first visit is ruling that out, and referring you on if that is the right call.

What happens at the first visit for back pain in Fort Collins?

The first visit is an exam that normally takes an hour, not a 10 or 15 minute stop. We take a detailed history and an ortho-neuro check with motion palpation, looking for tight muscles, inflammation, or a disc injury that needs more testing. In-clinic digital x-rays are used when pain is extreme or refers down the leg.

The history looks at when the pain started, where it is, and whether it is new or has been going on for years. From there, the exam helps us decide if we are comfortable starting care the first day, or if more testing is needed.

When we use digital x-ray, we are looking for degenerative change or arthritic conditions that may be contributing to the pain, especially with extreme pain or symptoms down the leg. For a fuller picture of the first visit, see our new patient exam in Fort Collins.

How does a Fort Collins chiropractor treat low back pain?

Care starts with finding the cause, then matching treatment to your goals. Alongside chiropractic care, we may use dry needling, spinal decompression for disc-related injuries or osteoarthritic change, shockwave therapy, massage therapy, and stretching that leads into full strength and rehabilitation. Home stretching and strength work are part of getting the result to hold.

Chiropractic care works on the mechanics of the problem. Specific adjustments restore movement to joints that have gotten stiff and guarded, which helps take pressure off irritated tissue and calm the pain. We start with a little frequency in treatment, then build from there.

Depending on what the exam shows, care may also include dry needling, spinal decompression for disc-related injuries or arthritis in the lower back, shockwave therapy, massage therapy, and passive and active stretching through strength and rehabilitation. The mix is chosen for your back, not as a fixed package.

How long does low back pain care take?

It depends on your goals. For some people, care can be as simple as three or four visits. If you want to get the problem resolved and work on strengthening, it can take two, three, or four months to truly rehabilitate a low back. After the exam, you choose the type of care that fits what you want to accomplish.

Some people want short-term, visit-to-visit care just to get the pain out of the area. Others choose relief care, typically two to six weeks, or a corrective style of care that rebuilds endurance and then dynamic strength, so everyday lifting is less likely to set the pain off again. You can read about those options on our types of care page.

When should I come in for low back pain?

The best time to come in is at the start of low back pain, rather than waiting to see if it will go away on its own. Coming in early gives the body less chance to develop compensatory patterns and muscle guarding, which can make treatment take longer and make it harder to recover.

Low back pain can be crippling, and it can keep you from the activities you love, whether it started yesterday or a long time ago. If the pain is severe or comes with numbness, weakness, or fever, call your medical doctor first. For common mechanical back pain, keep gently moving rather than staying flat in bed, and avoid the one heavy lift or twist that set it off, until you can be seen.

Around 80% of adults deal with low back pain at some point in their lives, which is why it is one of the most common reasons people seek care in the first place. You are far from alone in this, and it is one of the most workable things we see.

Source: U.S. National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH), Low-Back Pain and Complementary Health Approaches. nccih.nih.gov
