What causes low back pain?

Dr. Irwin sees it start in everyday Fort Collins life: raking leaves and yard work in the fall, bending and lifting to get ready for snow, or scooping snow in the front yard so it is safe to walk on. It can be something simple that started yesterday, or something that has been going on for a decade or two.

On the exam, he is looking for tight muscles and inflammation, and whether it is a disc injury that requires more testing or something he is comfortable working with day one. Digital x-rays in the clinic can help show degenerative change or arthritic conditions, especially when pain is extreme or refers down the leg.

What does the first visit look like?

The first visit is a full detailed history: when it started, where it is, and your past history, including whether it just happened or has been going on for years. From there he does a full ortho-neuro check with complete motion palpation. The examination normally takes an hour. In his words, a lot of clinics in Fort Collins will have you in and out in 10 or 15 minutes, fill out a sheet of paper, put you on a table, and start pushing down. That is not the approach he takes. He takes a deeper look at what is going on, trying to figure out the cause of the problem. Read about the new patient exam and report of findings.

How does care for low back pain work?

Most times care starts with a little bit of frequency. How long it lasts depends on your goal. It could be as simple as three or four visits, if that is what you want. If you truly want to get the problem resolved and work on strengthening, it could take upwards of two, three, or four months to truly rehabilitate it back to where it needs to be.

The clinic has a range of ways to work with low back pain:

  • Spinal decompression for disc-related injuries or osteoarthritic change, which is arthritis in the lower part of the back.
  • Shockwave therapy.
  • Massage therapy.
  • Full passive and active stretching and flexibility programs, worked through full strength and rehabilitation of a dysfunctional low back. See also physical rehabilitation.

The best thing you can do is catch it right when it starts, rather than waiting for it to go away. Coming in early gives the body less opportunity to develop compensatory patterns and muscle guarding, which make treatment take longer and recovery harder. See the types of care you can choose.
