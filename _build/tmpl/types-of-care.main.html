Visit-to-visit care

Some people just want the most cost-effective form of treatment, which is more just visit-to-visit type care, where the sole goal is to get the pain out of the area. Dr. Irwin definitely works with people like that. He also describes this as short-term acute pain management.

Relief care

Relief care is more anywhere from two to six weeks. A lot of times that type of care includes different modalities, like massage therapy, decompression, or SoftWave therapy, to try and get symptoms out of there as fast as possible.

The whole goal behind relief care is increasing range of motion, and decreasing muscle guarding and muscle spasm or muscle pain, to the point where you feel better.

Corrective care

Most patients want more than just symptoms gone. They don't want the symptoms to come back, and they want it to be stronger and healthier. Most patients will head into what he considers the corrective style of care. That involves rehabilitating the area with strength-based exercises.

He normally does endurance-based strength training first, and then works into dynamic strength training, which is more picking up weights, moving, bending, and twisting with it. The point is that the next time you reach down to pick up a laundry basket and put it on the washer, or pick a box up off the garage floor and set it on the shelf, your body has the strength to perform that action without throwing you right back into that acute state or that state of pain. See also physical rehabilitation.

What comes after, if you want it

A number of patients, after they have done the relief set of care and/or a full corrective form of care, choose to move on to wellness care, which is designed to maintain those results. Read about wellness care.

So at the end of the day, it is your choice on what type of care you do here, whether it is short-term acute pain management, or long-term relief of symptoms and corrective rehabilitative exercises. The goal is to get you as far along as you want to go, and back to as healthy as you want to be. That decision follows the new patient exam and, when needed, the report of findings.
